About

I'm a bioinformatics scientist at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, where I develop and clinically translate methods for finding structural and copy-number variants in genome sequencing data to help diagnose rare pediatric disease. Before that, I spent several years in cancer immunogenomics at Dana-Farber Cancer Institute and MD Anderson Cancer Center, studying why some patients respond exceptionally well to immunotherapy. That work led to co-first-author papers in Nature Cancer and Cancer Cell. I started my career in pharma R&D as a computational biology co-op at Novartis.

I like working end to end, from scoping a question with clinicians and scientists to building pipelines and models that change how they work.

Currently working on: structural variant detection and machine learning-based variant triage in clinical genome sequencing.

What I work on

Clinical genomics for rare pediatric disease

Methods and pipelines for structural and copy-number variant detection in clinical genome sequencing, built with clinical geneticists and genetic counselors to raise diagnostic yield.

Cancer immunogenomics

Why some patients respond exceptionally well to immune checkpoint inhibitors: clonal neoantigens, tertiary lymphoid structures, and molecular subtypes across phase III trials.

Machine learning for genomic interpretation

Deep learning and AutoML for triaging variant calls, with human-in-the-loop design so that no confirmed finding is dropped.
